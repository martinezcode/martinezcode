# Dinosaur Theme Park

## Ethical Considerations for Park Area 3

<!-- Header (End) -->

<!-- Body (Start) -->

**Introduction**

As a system architecture team leading the development of Park Area 3 with responsibility for the carnivorous dinosaurs, our goal is to provide amusement park services for an unparalleled customer experience. The team, along with the stakeholders involved in using the system, supporting the system, and paying for the system, understand that success requires consideration of the ethical issues surrounding information technology (IT).

**Ethical Issues Under Consideration**

*Responsible Disclosure*

- Collection of information is necessary for park operations. A breach of data in a cybersecurity incident could result in private information of employees or visitors being compromised. Laws, regulations, and/or ethical obligations may compel responsible disclosure of security incidents.

*Facial Recognition*

- Facial recognition is increasingly used for security purposes in areas where large crowds gather. The organization may benefit from utilizing this technology in the park, but must consider the laws, regulations, and/or ethical obligations surrounding facial recognition.

*Artificial Intelligence*

- Artificial intelligence (AI) may be used in security automation. The organization must consider the ethical implications of allowing machines to make decisions that affect security and/or safety of the organization and visitors.

*Sustainability of Resources*

- Public sentiment increasingly favors sustainable practices in energy consumption and use of natural resources. The organization must consider the environmental impact of park operations and be prepared to address questions about the sustainability of resources used to power park technology.

*Genetic Engineering*

- Genetic engineering technology enables recreation of extinct species and development of genetic hybrids such as the Indominus Rex, which is planned for inclusion in park area 3. The organization must define its own values in addition to considering the public\'s ethical perception of genetic engineering practices.

**Resolutions**

*Responsible Disclosure*

- The organization will have clear policies and procedures in place for handling information breaches and other cybersecurity incidents in a transparent and responsible manner.
- Stakeholders will work together to develop policies and procedures, including incident response plans with clearly defined actions and roles.
- Stakeholders will coordinate during and after any incidents to ensure response plans are followed.

*Facial Recognition*

- The organization will have clear policies and procedures in place for responsible collection and/or sharing of video information collected by security infrastructure.
- Stakeholders will work together to develop policies and procedures, including privacy policies and opt-in/opt-out mechanisms where required or otherwise appropriate.

*Artificial Intelligence*

- The organization will have clear policies and procedures in place for adoption and implementation of AI systems.
- Stakeholders will work together to develop policies and procedures, including risk assessment and incident response plans with clearly defined actions and roles.
- Stakeholders will work together to develop AI systems requirements documentation, with special consideration for failure conditions and decision making processes.

*Sustainability*

- The organization will have clear policies and procedures in place for resource usage and sourcing of consumable products.
- Stakeholders will work together to develop policies and procedures, including reporting of current sustainability practices and future goals.

*Genetic Engineering*

- The organization will have clear policies and procedures in place for genetic engineering practices.
- Stakeholders will work together to develop policies and procedures, including risk assessments, visitor sentiment analysis, and legal and regulatory compliance auditing.

**Responsibilites**

IT resources are critical elements of many park attractions, and they contribute directly to organizational security and visitor safety. To provide groundbreaking experiences, innovative technology is necessary. As IT pushes the limits of possibility, ethical questions arise. Unintended consequences and social sentiment must be taken into account. As suggested by Weinstein, ethical questions should be asked *before* taking action, such as:

- *Will this cause harm?*
- *Will this make things better?*
- *Will this respect others?*
- *Is this fair?*
- *Is this caring?*

**Conclusion**

In addition to recognizing ethical questions relative to its own core values, the organization should be aware of the impact of ethical *perception*. Attracting visitors from around the world brings a diverse set of views into the park. Cultural and social norms of guests must be respected. If actions or decisions are perceived as unethical, attendance will surely be impacted, which may lead to loss of revenue in addition to a loss of reputation.

However, by incorporating consideration of IT-related ethical issues into the early stages of park development, such risks can be mitigated or avoided entirely. A coordinated effort from all stakeholders, with clear guidance from the top of the organization, will create an environment built on an ethical foundation, and garner the public trust necessary for a flourishing dinosaur park.
