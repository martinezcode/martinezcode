# Project Based Risk Management Framework

## Facilitated Risk Analysis & Assessment Process (FRAAP) - Introduction

<!-- Header (End) -->

<!-- Body (Start) -->

**Introduction to Project Based Risk Management**

The Facilitated Risk Analysis and Assessment Process (FRAAP) framework is a collaborative risk management method focused on project-scale assessments conducted in a short time-frame. The method consists of one-time assessment session meetings involving project stakeholders and led by an Information Security facilitator to keep the meeting on track.

As noted by Wheeler, participants are encouraged to raise issues and identify risks, without side discussions and tangents. Once risks have been identified, team analyzes impacts and agrees on the likely consequences of risks. Then, each risk is rated in terms of the priority to the organization. A series of worksheets can be used to capture and prioritize the issues and risks discussed.

**Benefits of Project Based Risk Management**

- Risk assessment conducted in a short timeframe
- Structured & streamlined approach
- Accelerated assessment
- Cuts down the time to gather risk data and produce recommendations
- Good for one-time assessments

**Goals of Project Based Risk Management**

- Identification of risks and concerns
- Identification of threats
- Recommendations for controls to mitigate threats

**Conclusion**

The FRAAP approach is a good fit for teams with several existing software projects and regular introduction of new projects. The collaborative approach aligns with agile processes for brainstorming project ideas and could be easily incorporated into a Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> FRAAP is not meant for a company-wide assessment of all resources where a more extensive and less time-boxed framework such as the NIST Risk Management Framework would be more appropriate. However, FRAAP is a strong methodology for **project-based** risk management.
